The journal

Notes on strength, movement and living well in a woman's body.

Why your shoulders ache by 4 pm

Posture

A desk invites the body to fold forward: the head drifts ahead of the spine, the upper back rounds and the chest closes. The neck and shoulders then work overtime to hold your head up, which is why the ache arrives like clockwork.

Start small. Every hour, draw your shoulder blades gently together and down, lengthen the back of your neck and take three slow breaths. In class we go further, strengthening the muscles that hold you tall.

Strong after thirty: why jumping matters

Strength and bones

Bone responds to load. Gentle impact, such as skipping or small jumps, signals bones to stay dense, which becomes more valuable as the years pass. Muscle works alongside it, protecting joints and keeping daily life effortless.

If jumping is not right for you today, a low-impact variation delivers much of the same benefit. Progress is yours to pace.

Yoga is strength, not only softness

Yoga

Holding a pose asks for control, balance and breath, and builds strength in places a weights routine can miss. It is equally a daily reset for a busy mind.

Think of it as the thread that ties your week together: mobility for the body, quiet for the mind, and a reason to feel good before you have even finished.
